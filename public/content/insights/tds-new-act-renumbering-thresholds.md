From 1 April 2026, TDS and TCS are governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Most rates and thresholds are the same as before. What has changed is where the provisions sit, the forms, and how a payment is identified on challans and statements. This note is for anyone who deducts or collects tax: businesses, firms, employers, and individuals who must deduct TDS on rent or on a property purchase.

:::summary

### In short

- TDS on salary is under section 392, all other TDS under section 393, and TCS under section 394.
- Quarterly statements are filed in new forms: 138 (salary), 140 (other payments to residents), 144 (payments to non-residents) and 143 (TCS).
- A sum credited or paid on or before 31 March 2026 stays under the 1961 Act, even if the tax is deposited later.
- The Finance Act, 2026 changed some TCS rates but no TDS thresholds.
  :::

## Where the provisions now sit

The rules on deduction and collection of tax are in Part B of Chapter XIX of the new Act. Instead of a separate section for each kind of payment, they are set out in tables.

| Subject                                | 1961 Act        | 2025 Act      |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------- | ------------- |
| TDS on salary                          | 192             | 392           |
| TDS on payments to residents           | 193 to 194T     | 393(1), table |
| TDS on payments to non-residents       | 195 and others  | 393(2), table |
| Cases where no TDS is required         | various         | 393(4), table |
| TCS                                    | 206C            | 394           |
| Certificate for lower or nil deduction | 197             | 395           |
| TAN, deposit, statements, certificates | 203A, 200, 203  | 397           |
| Definitions, such as "work"            | 194C and others | 402           |

## Rates and thresholds

The rates and thresholds were carried into the new tables, and the Finance Act, 2026 did not change any TDS threshold. The thresholds raised from 1 April 2025 therefore still apply. Some of the common ones:

| Payment                                                           | Threshold                                     | Rate                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Interest from a bank, co-operative bank or post office            | ₹50,000 a year; ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens | 10%                                 |
| Other interest                                                    | ₹10,000 a year                                | 10%                                 |
| Rent of land, building or furniture                               | ₹50,000 a month                               | 10%                                 |
| Fees for professional services                                    | ₹50,000 a year                                | 10%                                 |
| Fees for technical services                                       | ₹50,000 a year                                | 2%                                  |
| Payments to contractors                                           | ₹30,000 a payment or ₹1,00,000 a year         | 1% (individual or HUF), 2% (others) |
| Commission or brokerage                                           | ₹20,000 a year                                | 2%                                  |
| Salary, commission, bonus or interest paid by a firm to a partner | ₹20,000 a year                                | 10%                                 |

Three points to note:

- **Rent paid by individuals.** Individuals and HUFs who are not liable to tax audit deduct 2% on rent above ₹50,000 a month (the old section 194-IB), not 10%.
- **Manpower supply.** The Finance Act, 2026 added the supply of manpower to the definition of "work" from 1 April 2026. Payments for it now attract TDS at the contractor rates of 1% or 2%.
- **Payments to partners.** TDS on a firm's payments to its partners has applied since 1 April 2025. Many firms had no history of deducting tax on these payments and should check that they now do.

The higher rates for non-filers (old sections 206AB and 206CCA) and TCS on the sale of goods (old section 206C(1H)) were withdrawn from 1 April 2025 and have not returned.

## TCS changes from 1 April 2026

The Finance Act, 2026 changed several TCS rates:

- On liquor, scrap and minerals, TCS went up from 1% to 2%.
- On tendu leaves, it came down from 5% to 2%.
- On foreign remittances under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme above ₹10 lakh, it is 2% for education and medical treatment and 20% for other purposes.
- On overseas tour packages, it is a flat 2%.

## Challans, statements and certificates

- **Payment codes.** On the challan and in the quarterly statement, the nature of payment is now chosen from a list of codes instead of a section number. Take the codes from the e-filing portal and the current version of the statement preparation utility rather than from lists circulating online.
- **New form numbers.** Quarterly TDS statements: Form 24Q is now 138, 26Q is 140 and 27Q is 144. The TCS statement, 27EQ, is now 143. Certificates: Form 16 is now 130 and 16A is 131. Form 16B to 16E (property, rent and similar) is now 132, and Form 27D is 133. The declaration for no deduction (15G or 15H) is now Form 121, and the application for a lower deduction certificate is Form 128.
- **Due dates.** Tax deducted is due by the 7th of the following month, and by 30 April for March. The statement for July to September 2026 is due by 31 October 2026.
- **Corrections.** A statement filed under the new Act can be corrected only within two years from the end of the tax year. Statements for periods under the 1961 Act are corrected under the old rules.
- **Lower deduction certificates.** A certificate issued under the old section 197 for receipts of tax year 2026-27 continues to be valid. New applications are made under section 395 in Form 128.
- **Buying property from a non-resident.** From 1 October 2026, a resident individual or HUF who buys property from a non-resident does not need a TAN to deduct and pay the tax.

## Payments around 31 March 2026

Which Act applies depends on when the tax was deductible, which is on credit or payment, whichever is earlier. If an expense was credited to the payee's account on or before 31 March 2026, the 1961 Act applies even if the payment is made later. If tax was deducted on credit under the old Act, it is not deducted again when the payment is made.

## What to check now

- Payroll and accounting software use the new sections, payment codes and form numbers for payments from 1 April 2026.
- Contracts for the supply of manpower are treated as work.
- Firms deduct tax on payments to partners above ₹20,000 a year.
- Vendor masters reflect the current thresholds, so that tax is neither missed nor deducted where it is not needed.
