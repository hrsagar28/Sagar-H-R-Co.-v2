The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 on 1 April 2026. It rewrites and renumbers the law but does not bring in a new tax. This note covers which Act applies to which year, the move to a single "tax year", and what has and has not changed.

:::summary

### In short

- The Income-tax Act, 2025 (Act No. 30 of 2025) came into force on 1 April 2026 and repealed the 1961 Act.
- Income up to 31 March 2026 stays under the 1961 Act, including returns and proceedings for those years started after that date.
- "Previous year" and "assessment year" are replaced by one "tax year". The first under the new Act is tax year 2026-27.
- Most sections and forms have new numbers. Slab rates for tax year 2026-27 are the same as for FY 2025-26.
  :::

## Which Act applies to which year

Income earned up to 31 March 2026, that is FY 2025-26 and earlier years, is governed by the 1961 Act. The return for FY 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27), although filed in 2026, uses the old sections and the old forms.

Assessments, appeals, rectifications and other proceedings for those years continue under the 1961 Act. Under section 536 of the new Act, this also applies to proceedings for those years that begin after 1 April 2026.

Income earned from 1 April 2026 falls under the 2025 Act, starting with tax year 2026-27.

For TDS, what matters is when the tax was deductible, which is on credit or payment, whichever is earlier. A sum credited or paid on or before 31 March 2026 stays under the 1961 Act even if the tax is deposited later.

## One tax year instead of two

Under the 1961 Act, income earned in a "previous year" was taxed in the following "assessment year". Income of FY 2024-25, for example, was assessed in assessment year 2025-26.

The 2025 Act uses one term. Section 3 defines the tax year as the twelve months of the financial year beginning on 1 April. Income is still assessed after the year ends; only the second label has gone. Income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 is income of tax year 2026-27.

For a business or profession set up during the year, or a new source of income, the first tax year begins on the date it is set up or arises and ends on 31 March.

## What has changed

**Section numbers.** The new Act has 536 sections, against 819 in the 1961 Act. Almost every provision has moved:

| Provision                              | 1961 Act                        | 2025 Act |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------- |
| New tax regime                         | 115BAC                          | 202      |
| Deduction for LIC, PF, PPF and similar | 80C                             | 123      |
| Deduction for health insurance         | 80D                             | 126      |
| Return of income                       | 139                             | 263      |
| Tax audit                              | 44AB                            | 63       |
| Presumptive taxation                   | 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE               | 58       |
| Capital gains (charging section)       | 45                              | 67       |
| TDS on salary                          | 192                             | 392      |
| TDS on other payments                  | 193 to 196D (about 40 sections) | 393      |
| TCS                                    | 206C                            | 394      |

**Exemptions.** The list in section 10 of the old Act is now spread across Schedules II to VI, and the persons and bodies whose income is exempt are in Schedule VII. Some salary items, such as gratuity, commuted pension and leave encashment, are now deductions from salary under section 19.

**Rules and forms.** The Income-tax Rules, 2026, notified on 20 March 2026, replace the 1962 Rules. They cut the number of rules from 511 to 333 and of forms from 399 to 190, and renumber the forms. Some changes from tax year 2026-27:

| Form                               | Old           | New |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------- | --- |
| TDS certificate for salary         | 16            | 130 |
| TDS certificate for other payments | 16A           | 131 |
| Annual tax statement               | 26AS          | 168 |
| Declaration for no TDS             | 15G, 15H      | 121 |
| Tax audit report                   | 3CA, 3CB, 3CD | 26  |
| Application for lower TDS          | 13            | 128 |

Forms for FY 2025-26 and earlier years stay as they were.

## What has not changed

- **Slab rates.** The rates for tax year 2026-27 are the same as for FY 2025-26, and the new tax regime remains the default.
- **The basic scheme.** The heads of income, the residence rules and the way total income is computed are carried forward.
- **PAN and TAN.** Existing PAN and TAN continue. No fresh registration is needed.
- **Deductions.** Deductions such as those under the old sections 80C and 80D continue under the old regime, under new numbers.
- **Registrations and approvals.** Existing registrations and approvals, such as those of trusts under the old sections 12AB and 80G, are treated as granted under the new Act.
- **Losses.** Brought-forward losses and unabsorbed depreciation carry over to the new Act.
- **TDS deposit dates.** Tax deducted is still due by the 7th of the following month, and by 30 April for March.

## What to update

Most of the work is in references and systems rather than tax. Payroll and accounting software, TDS settings, engagement letters and agreements that quote section numbers need updating for transactions from 1 April 2026. Before quoting a new section number in a filing or reply, check it against the text of the Act.
